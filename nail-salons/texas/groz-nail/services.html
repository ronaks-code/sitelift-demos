Questions

Frequently asked

What is a Russian manicure?

A detailed manicure where the cuticle area is cleaned with an electric file instead of only pushing and clipping. Done carefully, it lets the gel sit very close to the cuticle, so the manicure looks clean and grows out neatly.

How much does it cost?

A Signature Russian Manicure is $70 without gel and $120 with gel on short to medium nails ($145 for long nails). First visit? The New Client Russian Manicure is $90 and starts with a consultation. The full menu is on the Services page.

Does the e-file hurt?

It should not. Several clients mention they were nervous about the e-file and found Julia extremely delicate and careful. Speak up right away if anything feels uncomfortable and she will adjust.

What is a dry pedicure?

The Signature Russian Pedicure is done without soaking in water: thorough cuticle care, shaping and callus treatment with a Smart Disc, with or without gel.

Can you fix a bad manicure or pedicure?

Yes. Clients have come in with an at-home gel nightmare or a pedicure that went wrong elsewhere and left with a clean, corrected set. Removal of gel ($15) or acrylic and dip ($25) from another salon is booked as a separate add-on.

Are there any offers?

Yes: 10% off your first visit, $15 off during your birthday month, and $20 off every Wednesday (limited availability).

How often should I come back?

Julia recommends refreshing gel about every 4 weeks. In between, use cuticle oil and avoid picking or peeling the gel.

How do I book?

Groz.Nail is by appointment only. Book online to see every open time; designs are add-ons, so pick them when you book so extra time is set aside.
